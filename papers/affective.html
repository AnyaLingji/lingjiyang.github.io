COURSEWORK / Experimental pre-analysis plan

Affective vs Cognitive AI in Digital Intimacy

A Pre-Analysis Plan for Understanding the Impact of Chatbot Response Style on Trust, Intimacy, and Disclosure
Forecasting Methods and Causal Inference for the Social SciencesVG · Pass with Distinction2025
01 / OVERVIEW

What this work explores

A proposed 14-day randomized experiment asks whether supportive and critical chatbot responses affect women’s willingness to disclose personal information and how attachment style might change those effects.

02 / RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Questions

  1. Does supportive versus critical AI response style change willingness to self-disclose?
  2. Do anxious, avoidant, and secure attachment styles moderate the response?
  3. Do trust, social presence, intimacy, and emotional safety differ across conditions?
03 / APPROACH

Research design & material

The paper specifies a between-subjects randomized design involving approximately 100 experienced female chatbot users assigned to one of two response styles over 14 days. It develops hypotheses, operational measures, and an analysis strategy including OLS regression, bootstrapped mediation, and causal forests. Course-provided R scripts and simulated data were used to explore expected patterns; no human participant experiment was conducted.

04 / THEORETICAL LENS

Conceptual foundations

The theoretical foundation draws on the CASA paradigm, social presence, self-disclosure and attachment theory to explain why emotionally affirming versus cognitively challenging AI responses may produce different interpersonal perceptions.

05 / VISUAL ARCHIVE

Visualizing the research

Proposed two-condition study design; no trial with participants was conducted.
06 / ANALYSIS

Key arguments & findings

Response-style contrast

Affective messages are designed to validate feelings and encourage disclosure; cognitive messages challenge assumptions and emphasize reflective reasoning.

Proposed mechanisms

Trust, perceived intimacy, social presence, emotional safety and perceived substitutability are specified as possible pathways connecting response style and disclosure.

Simulation findings

Simulated analyses suggested greater disclosure under supportive responses and possible differences by attachment orientation. These are demonstrations based on artificial data, not evidence of real-world treatment effects.

07 / ORIGINAL WORK

Contribution

The contribution is an executable and theoretically specified research design linking conversational AI response style to disclosure, attachment and psychological mechanisms, together with a reproducible simulation-based analysis strategy.

This is a student research paper, not a peer-reviewed journal publication. Concept diagrams summarize the manuscript and do not constitute additional empirical evidence.
