SELECTED COURSEWORK · UPPSALA UNIVERSITY · 28 April 2025

Affective vs Cognitive AI in Digital Intimacy

A Pre-Analysis Plan for Understanding the Impact of Chatbot Response Style on Trust, Intimacy, and Disclosure
Forecasting Methods and Causal Inference for the Social SciencesHuman–AI Interaction · Experimental Design · Causal Inference

Lingji Yang · 28 April 2025

01 / Overview

How does the communication style of an AI chatbot shape women's willingness to share personal thoughts and emotions?

This project develops a pre-analysis plan for investigating the effects of supportive (affective) and critical (cognitive) chatbot responses on self-disclosure in digitally mediated intimate interactions.

It examines whether supportive communication is associated with greater willingness to disclose, how users' attachment styles may moderate this relationship, and whether psychological experiences such as trust, social presence, intimacy, and emotional safety help explain the proposed effect.

The research combines communication theory, attachment theory, experimental design, and simulation-based statistical analysis in R.

02 / Research Questions

RQ1 · Response Style and Self-Disclosure

Does the response style of AI chatbots, supportive (affective) versus critical (cognitive), significantly influence female users' willingness to engage in self-disclosure during digital intimate interactions?

RQ2 · Attachment Style

Do female users with different attachment styles respond differently to chatbot response styles in terms of disclosure?

RQ3 · Psychological Experience

Does chatbot response style differentially influence female users' experiences of trust, social presence, intimacy, and emotional safety during interaction?

03 / Theoretical Framework

Computers Are Social Actors

The CASA paradigm provides a foundation for understanding why people may respond socially to conversational technologies and attribute relational characteristics to AI systems.

Social Presence Theory

Social presence theory helps explain how the perceived presence of a responsive conversational partner may encourage emotional engagement and self-disclosure.

Attachment Theory

Attachment theory introduces individual differences in relational expectations and emotional needs.

The study considers three attachment orientations: anxious, avoidant, and secure. These orientations may shape users' responses to supportive and critical chatbot communication.

04 / Proposed Experimental Design

The research specifies a 14-day, between-subjects randomised experiment involving approximately 100 female participants with previous experience using AI chatbots.

Participants would be assigned to one of two conditions.

Supportive / Affective Condition

The chatbot uses warmth, empathy, reassurance, and emotionally validating language.

Critical / Cognitive Condition

The chatbot provides analytical, rational, and informationally clear responses, with an emphasis on constructive feedback.

The proposed interaction period contains two phases:

Days 1–6: Acquaintance Building

Participants would discuss personal interests, everyday emotions, self-introductions, and minor difficulties.

Days 7–14: Emotional Deepening

The conversations would move toward more personal experiences, fears, regrets, aspirations, and relationship expectations.

The proposed design includes daily conversations, interim and final questionnaires, and measures of participants' attachment styles.

The experiment was designed but not implemented with recruited participants as part of this project.

05 / Hypotheses

H1 · Main Effect

Supportive chatbot responses are expected to increase willingness to self-disclose compared with critical chatbot responses.

H2 · Moderating Role of Attachment Style

Attachment orientation is expected to influence how participants respond to different chatbot communication styles.

The study predicts stronger disclosure under supportive conditions among anxiously attached users and explores whether avoidantly attached users respond relatively more favourably to critical communication.

H3 · Psychological Mediators

Trust, social presence, intimacy, and emotional safety are proposed as psychological mechanisms linking chatbot response style with willingness to disclose.

06 / Statistical Analysis

The project includes statistical analyses conducted in R using simulated data.

Three analytical approaches were applied.

Ordinary Least Squares Regression

OLS regression was used to estimate the simulated relationship between chatbot response style and self-disclosure willingness.

Bootstrapped Mediation Analysis

Mediation analyses with 5,000 bootstrap resamples examined the potential indirect effects of trust, social presence, intimacy, emotional safety, and substitutability.

Causal Forests

Causal Forest analysis explored heterogeneous treatment effects associated with attachment styles and other participant characteristics.

These analyses demonstrate the application of quantitative methods to the proposed study design. The results are based on simulated observations rather than data collected from actual participants.

07 / Simulation Results

Main Effect

The OLS model produced an estimated treatment coefficient of 3.056, indicating higher simulated self-disclosure willingness in the supportive condition.

Psychological Mediators

Trust, social presence, and intimacy showed statistically significant mediation effects within the simulated dataset.

Emotional safety and substitutability did not show statistically significant mediation effects.

Attachment Style

The Causal Forest analysis estimated positive treatment effects across attachment groups, with the highest estimated effect among users with anxious attachment.

The simulation did not support the prediction that avoidantly attached users would respond more favourably to critical communication.

These findings illustrate the analytical procedures and possible hypothesis patterns. They do not establish empirical effects of chatbot communication styles on real users.

08 / Discussion

The project connects conversational AI design with questions of emotional disclosure, relational expectations, and perceived intimacy.

It considers how supportive communication may influence users' experiences of trust and social presence, and how attachment orientations might shape these processes.

The simulation exercise also highlights the methodological distinction between a theoretically expected relationship and an empirically demonstrated effect.

Future research would require implementing the proposed experiment, recruiting participants, collecting behavioural and survey data, and evaluating whether the simulated relationships are observed in real interactions.

09 / Conclusion

This project develops a quantitative research design for examining how chatbot response styles may influence women's willingness to self-disclose.

By combining experimental planning with regression, mediation analysis, and exploratory heterogeneous treatment-effect estimation, it establishes a methodological foundation for studying AI-mediated intimacy and relational communication.

The proposed relationships remain to be tested through empirical research.
