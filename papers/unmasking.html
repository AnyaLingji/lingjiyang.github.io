COURSEWORK / CRITICAL MEDIA ANALYSIS · OCT 2024

Unmasking Digital Intimacy

A Critical Analysis of Emotion, Algorithms, and Economics
Digital Media, Culture and SocietyCourse grade not linked to this essayCourse essay
01 / OVERVIEW

What this work explores

Through romantic gaming, AI chatbots and paid cosplay encounters, this essay examines how emotional interaction can become an object of design, platform monetization and user consumption.

02 / RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Questions

  1. How do digital platforms and affective technologies manufacture experiences of intimacy?
  2. What roles do personalization, user disclosure and economic incentives play in shaping these relationships?
03 / APPROACH

Research design & material

The essay combines interdisciplinary literature with critical examination of three cases: Love and Deepspace, XingyeAI, and commissioned cosplay services in China. This is a critical conceptual and case-based analysis; the essay does not report an independently recruited sample or controlled experiment.

04 / THEORETICAL LENS

Conceptual foundations

Its interpretation draws on the affective turn, parasocial interaction, emotional labour, emotional capitalism and critiques of algorithmic opacity to examine the relationship between personal feeling and commercial systems.

06 / ANALYSIS

Key arguments & findings

Emotional fulfillment

Digital companions and games may provide accessible, predictable experiences of closeness and recognition.

Platform incentives

Interfaces invite self-disclosure and personalization while translating attention and emotion into monetizable interaction.

Productive consumption

The essay argues that users both help produce personalized emotional experiences through disclosure and consume these same experiences as paid or commercially valuable products.

07 / ORIGINAL WORK

Contribution

The essay advances a critical reading of digital intimacy as a technologically organized and commercially mediated experience, connecting emotional dependency, platform design and economic extraction.

This is a student research paper, not a peer-reviewed journal publication. Concept diagrams summarize the manuscript and do not constitute additional empirical evidence.
