COURSEWORK / Reflective learning diary · theoretical essay

Toward the Other

Theory, Morality, and Body
Advanced Study of Anthropological TheoryVG · Pass with Distinction2025
01 / OVERVIEW

What this work explores

A reflective exploration of encounter, ethical responsibility and embodiment, connecting major anthropological debates to human–AI relationships and the limits of digital bodies.

02 / RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Questions

  1. How can researchers encounter others without reducing them to objects of knowledge?
  2. What are the ethical limits of observation, representation and intervention?
  3. What happens to the meanings of embodiment and otherness when technology mediates experience?
03 / APPROACH

Research design & material

The work is structured as a reflective learning diary engaging lectures, seminars and anthropological readings. Its sections examine theories of encountering the Other, the morality of encounter and the body. A final task extends those discussions to AI-mediated intimacy. It is conceptual reflection rather than original empirical fieldwork.

04 / THEORETICAL LENS

Conceptual foundations

It engages Martin Buber’s I–Thou encounter, anthropological debates over cultural relativism, the ethical turn, embodiment, posthuman concerns, Foucault’s technologies of the self, and Deleuze and Guattari’s concepts of desire and assemblage.

06 / ANALYSIS

Key arguments & findings

Theory and alterity

The diary traces how understanding others requires attention to the power relations involved in naming, representing and interpreting cultural difference.

Ethics and responsibility

Research encounters demand reflection on the tension between description, moral judgment and intervention.

Embodiment and AI

The final sections ask how technologically mediated sensation and artificial bodies challenge received notions of self, Other and embodied experience.

07 / ORIGINAL WORK

Contribution

The learning diary develops an interdisciplinary theoretical vocabulary that later informs the investigation of desire, vulnerability, digital bodies and subjectivity in the master’s thesis.

This is a student research paper, not a peer-reviewed journal publication. Concept diagrams summarize the manuscript and do not constitute additional empirical evidence.
