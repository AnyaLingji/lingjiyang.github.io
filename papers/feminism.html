Subverted from Within

The Toxic Turn and Its Repercussions for Chinese Feminism

How Chilling Effects Undermine Feminism Movements

Global Perspectives on Social Change and Digital MediaDigital Feminism · Social Movements · Chilling Effects · Narrative Literature Review

Jia He & Lingji Yang · 20 February 2025

01 / Overview

Digital platforms have provided important spaces for feminist activism in China, allowing women to articulate shared grievances, challenge gender inequality, and participate in collective discussions.

Yet these spaces have also become sites of ideological conflict, exclusionary discourse, and increasing political and commercial pressure.

This research examines what the paper terms the toxic turn in Chinese feminism between 2019 and 2025, focusing on how feminist discourse becomes fragmented, appropriated, and contested within digital environments.

Rather than attributing the challenges facing Chinese feminist movements solely to external repression, the review investigates how internal contradictions and conflicts may also undermine solidarity, participation, and collective mobilisation.

The paper draws on a narrative literature review of 20 peer-reviewed studies to examine these dynamics and their implications for the sustainability of feminist activism.

02 / Research Questions

RQ1 · The Toxic Turn

How has the Chinese feminist movement shifted from constructive activism to internal toxicity, marked by co-optation, factionalism, and the stigmatization of feminist identities?

RQ2 · Chilling Effects

In what ways has this toxic turn contributed to a chilling effect, limiting feminist participation and mobilization?

03 / Theoretical Perspective

Emancipatory and Retrogressive Forces

The study draws on Liinason and Norocel's (2024) Double Helix Model, which conceptualises emancipatory and retrogressive technocultural forces as interconnected and continually evolving in relation to one another.

Building on this framework, the paper argues that an emancipatory movement can contain internal contradictions.

Within Chinese digital feminism, efforts to challenge gender inequality coexist with practices that may reproduce exclusion, hierarchy, and ideological policing.

This perspective provides a basis for examining how feminist movements negotiate tensions between collective liberation and internal fragmentation.

Chilling Effects

The concept of chilling effects helps explain how perceived risks and negative consequences can discourage people from exercising their rights to expression and participation.

The paper examines chilling effects across two dimensions:

Behavioural: Withdrawal from feminist discussions, collective activities, or public identification with feminism.

Content: Self-censorship, changes in language, avoidance of sensitive topics, and movement toward less visible forms of expression.

04 / Research Method

Narrative Literature Review

The study employs a traditional narrative literature review to synthesise scholarship on Chinese feminist discourse, digital activism, internal conflict, and chilling effects.

Literature was retrieved from Web of Science, ScienceDirect, and Scopus, focusing on peer-reviewed studies published between 2019 and 2025.

The search initially identified 2,985 records. Following duplicate removal and preliminary screening, approximately 65 articles underwent full-text review.

A final selection of 20 peer-reviewed studies formed the basis of the analysis.

Matrix-Based Thematic Analysis

An analytical matrix was developed to organise the selected literature, recording publication details, keywords, and principal findings.

Through reading, comparison, and thematic synthesis, the studies were grouped into three overarching themes:

  1. The Co-optation and Commercialisation of Feminist Discourse
  2. Intra-Movement Conflicts and Exclusionary Practices
  3. Chilling Effects and Their Impact on Mobilisation
05 / Key Findings

01. The Co-optation and Commercialisation of Feminist Discourse

The review identifies two interconnected processes through which feminist discourse can be redirected away from collective political transformation.

State Co-optation

Feminist language may be selectively incorporated into official narratives while grassroots activism challenging structural inequality faces restrictions.

In this process, women's empowerment can be reframed around state-sanctioned objectives, limiting the space available for more transformative feminist demands.

Market-Driven Feminism

Commercial interests can reposition feminist empowerment as a matter of individual consumption, self-improvement, beauty, and professional success.

Drawing on the mechanisms of aestheticisation, corporatisation, and subjectification, the paper examines how feminist discourse becomes incorporated into advertising, popular culture, and influencer economies.

These processes risk substituting individual consumer choice for collective political action.

02. Intra-Movement Conflicts and Exclusionary Practices

The second theme concerns ideological divisions and disputes over feminist legitimacy.

Different feminist positions have developed competing understandings of marriage, heterosexual relationships, economic independence, reproductive choices, and women's participation in consumer culture.

Such disagreements can become mechanisms of boundary policing when particular groups claim authority to determine who qualifies as a legitimate feminist.

The review also discusses exclusionary practices affecting married women, mothers, LGBTQ+ communities, and others whose experiences or choices are deemed incompatible with particular feminist positions.

These conflicts reveal how movements seeking to challenge gender hierarchy may themselves reproduce forms of exclusion.

03. Chilling Effects and Their Impact on Mobilisation

The third theme examines how internal hostility and external pressures can combine to discourage participation.

Behavioural Dimension: Feminism Avoidance

Some women may support gender equality while avoiding public identification with feminism.

Fear of criticism, ideological policing, social stigma, and conflict can make participation appear personally costly.

This withdrawal can weaken solidarity and reduce the movement's capacity to mobilise.

Content Dimension: Language Compromise and Private Retreat

The review identifies pressures including platform censorship, hostile media framing, government restrictions, and networked misogyny.

In response, feminist participants may moderate their language, avoid controversial issues, or move discussions into more private spaces.

Together, these behavioural and content-level changes contribute to a chilling effect that limits the visibility and continuity of feminist activism.

06 / Discussion

The findings suggest that the fragmentation of Chinese digital feminism cannot be understood exclusively through external political constraints.

The review argues that state appropriation, market commercialisation, ideological conflict, and exclusionary practices interact with censorship and public hostility to weaken collective action.

These processes may reinforce one another.

Commercialisation can redirect attention toward individual empowerment, while ideological disputes narrow the boundaries of acceptable feminist participation. The resulting conflicts may encourage disengagement and self-censorship, further reducing opportunities for sustained collective mobilisation.

Building on the Double Helix Model, the paper highlights the coexistence of emancipatory ambitions and potentially exclusionary practices within a movement.

This tension is central to understanding the challenges of maintaining solidarity across diverse feminist positions.

07 / Conclusion

The review identifies three interconnected challenges facing Chinese digital feminism:

Co-optation and Commercialisation: Feminist discourse can be redirected toward state objectives and market interests.

Internal Fragmentation: Factional conflict and exclusionary practices may undermine solidarity across feminist communities.

Chilling Effects: Self-censorship, withdrawal, and restrictions on expression can weaken participation and mobilisation.

Together, these dynamics constrain the transformative potential of feminist activism.

Nevertheless, the paper recognises that feminist discourse continues to generate awareness of gender inequality and create possibilities for social change.

It concludes by raising questions about how new alliances, intersectional approaches, and more inclusive forms of collective organisation might help sustain feminist movements amid changing social and political conditions.
