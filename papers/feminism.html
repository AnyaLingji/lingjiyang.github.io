COURSEWORK / Collaborative narrative literature review

Subverted from Within

The Toxic Turn and Its Repercussions for Chinese Feminism: How Chilling Effects Undermine Feminism Movements
Global Perspectives on Social Change and Digital MediaVG · Pass with Distinction2025
01 / OVERVIEW

What this work explores

A collaborative review of 20 studies examines how commercialization, ideological disputes and exclusionary practices may create chilling effects within contemporary Chinese feminism.

02 / RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Questions

  1. How do co-optation, commercialization and conflicts within feminist spaces contribute to fragmentation?
  2. How do these dynamics relate to self-censorship, disengagement and constraints on feminist mobilization?
03 / APPROACH

Research design & material

Co-authored with Jia He. The paper uses a narrative literature review and matrix-based thematic analysis of 20 selected studies published in 2019–2025, retrieved through Web of Science, ScienceDirect and Scopus. It organizes evidence into three overarching themes. Its conclusions are interpretive synthesis rather than direct field observations of activists.

04 / THEORETICAL LENS

Conceptual foundations

The discussion draws on the Double Helix Model of emancipatory and retrogressive technocultural worldings to examine how countervailing forces may coexist inside movements that pursue social emancipation.

05 / VISUAL ARCHIVE

Visualizing the research

Conceptual summary of reviewed themes, not a measured causal sequence.
06 / ANALYSIS

Key arguments & findings

Co-optation and commercialization

State and market interests may repurpose feminist discourse into governance-friendly or consumption-oriented narratives.

Intra-movement conflicts

Disputes over legitimacy, identity and participation can foster exclusion and reduce solidarity among participants.

Chilling effects

The paper distinguishes behavioural withdrawal from content-level self-censorship, arguing that internal conflict interacts with external repression to constrain participation.

07 / ORIGINAL WORK

Contribution

The review reframes feminist mobilization as shaped by external constraints and internal tensions together, connecting the politics of digital discourse to participation and movement sustainability.

This is a student research paper, not a peer-reviewed journal publication. Concept diagrams summarize the manuscript and do not constitute additional empirical evidence.
