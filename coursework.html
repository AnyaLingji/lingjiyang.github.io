Affective vs Cognitive AI in Digital Intimacy

Forecasting Methods and Causal Inference · VG · 2025
A Pre-Analysis Plan for Understanding the Impact of Chatbot Response Style on Trust, Intimacy, and Disclosure

A proposed 14-day randomized experiment asks whether supportive and critical chatbot responses affect women’s willingness to disclose personal information and how attachment style might change those effects.

RESEARCH OUTPUT
The contribution is an executable and theoretically specified research design linking conversational AI response style to disclosure, attachment and psychological mechanisms, together with a reproducible simulation-based analysis strategy.

Between Healing and Harm

Health Psychology and Behavioural Medicine · VG · 2025
A Dual-Pathway–Three-Dimension Model of Digital Companionship and Mental Health

Drawing on 21 selected studies, this paper proposes a conceptual model of how AI companionship may provide emotional support or, under other patterns of use, undermine psychological flexibility.

RESEARCH OUTPUT
The paper contributes the Dual-Pathway–Three-Dimension Model, a conceptual synthesis intended to guide research and design on emotional support, dependence and psychological flexibility. The proposed pathways should not be interpreted as experimentally established causal effects.

Subverted from Within

Global Perspectives on Social Change and Digital Media · VG · 2025
The Toxic Turn and Its Repercussions for Chinese Feminism: How Chilling Effects Undermine Feminism Movements

A collaborative review of 20 studies examines how commercialization, ideological disputes and exclusionary practices may create chilling effects within contemporary Chinese feminism.

RESEARCH OUTPUT
The review reframes feminist mobilization as shaped by external constraints and internal tensions together, connecting the politics of digital discourse to participation and movement sustainability.

Unmasking Digital Intimacy

Digital Media, Culture and Society · VG · Oct 2024 · 7.5 ECTS
A Critical Analysis of Emotion, Algorithms, and Economics

Through romantic gaming, AI chatbots and paid cosplay encounters, this essay examines how emotional interaction can become an object of design, platform monetization and user consumption.

RESEARCH OUTPUT
The essay advances a critical reading of digital intimacy as a technologically organized and commercially mediated experience, connecting emotional dependency, platform design and economic extraction.
