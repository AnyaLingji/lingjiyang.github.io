Digital Transgression

Chinese Women's Kink Interactions with Generative AI

Generative AI is emerging as a space for intimate and erotic experimentation. This thesis examines how Chinese women engage with generative AI in kink-related interactions, exploring desires and relational experiences that may be difficult to express, negotiate, or enact in everyday life.

01 / OVERVIEW

Generative AI is emerging as a space for intimate and erotic experimentation. This thesis examines how Chinese women engage with generative AI in kink-related interactions, exploring desires and relational experiences that may be difficult to express, negotiate, or enact in everyday life.

The study approaches these interactions through digital transgression, understood as mediated existential practices in which subjects approach, test, cross, inhabit, and sometimes redraw boundaries to explore possibilities of being beyond the everyday self.

Rather than evaluating these experiences in advance as liberating or harmful, the research investigates how women encounter social, relational, and technological limits, how AI mediates their movement across these boundaries, and how such encounters reshape their understandings of desire, vulnerability, and personal agency.

Research at a glance

  • 9 participants
  • 18 in-depth interviews across two rounds
  • Contextual netnography on RedNote
  • Existential phenomenological analysis
02 / RESEARCH QUESTIONS

RQ1. What social, relational, and technological boundaries do women encounter and actively approach in kink-related intimate interactions with generative AI?

RQ2. How do women enact and experience transgression through the language generation, character configuration, interactional mechanisms, and broader media conditions of generative AI?

RQ3. How do these experiences of digital transgression reshape participants' understandings of their desires, subject positions, and personal boundaries, and what existential possibilities and risks beyond the everyday self do they open?

03 / THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

The thesis brings together three interconnected theoretical perspectives to examine digital transgression as an existential media phenomenon.

Late Modernity and the Demand for Self-Realisation

Drawing on Hartmut Rosa, the study situates women's intimate AI practices within conditions of social acceleration, self-management, and the pressure to pursue self-realisation.

This perspective provides a social context for understanding why experiences that temporarily suspend everyday responsibilities, expectations, and demands for self-coherence may become meaningful.

Erotic Transgression

Georges Bataille's theory of erotic transgression provides the conceptual basis for examining encounters with desire that disturb established boundaries.

The analysis draws on four interconnected dimensions:

  • Nudity: Bringing difficult desires into language and exposing aspects of the self.
  • Obscenity: Encountering desires that unsettle existing moral and social judgments.
  • Dissolution: Moving across vulnerability, control, submission, and unstable subject positions.
  • Expenditure: Allowing desire and intensity to remain meaningful within the experience itself.

Existential Media

Amanda Lagerkvist's existential media theory provides a framework for understanding how technological mediation reorganises fundamental conditions of human existence.

The analysis focuses on vulnerability, ambivalence, limits, and transcendence, examining how generative AI simultaneously creates possibilities for intimate exploration and introduces new forms of uncertainty and dependence.

Together, these perspectives inform an existential media framework for analysing digital transgression.

04 / METHODOLOGY

The research followed a qualitative design combining contextual netnography, in-depth interviews, and existential phenomenological analysis.

Phase One: Contextual Netnography

The study began with an examination of publicly circulated AI persona scripts and related discussions on Xiaohongshu (RedNote).

Platform materials were collected between 7 and 24 October 2025. An initial screening identified 89 relevant posts, subsequently narrowed to a core corpus of 27 posts published by 25 creators.

This phase provided contextual knowledge of AI persona-construction practices, supported recruitment, and informed the interview inquiry.

The platform corpus was not treated as a direct representation of its creators' private desires or as a dataset equivalent to the interview material.

Phase Two: In-Depth Interviews

Nine Chinese women with direct experience of AI-mediated kink-related interactions participated in two rounds of interviews.

The first round invited open-ended descriptions of participants' lived experiences. The second returned to experiences and meanings requiring further clarification.

Together, the 18 interviews provided the primary experiential material for the study.

Phase Three: Existential Phenomenological Analysis

Following Churchill's existential phenomenological research approach, the interview transcripts were analysed through meaning-unit analysis and the development of individual structural descriptions (ISDs).

Cross-case analysis then produced a general structural description (GSD) of the phenomenon.

The analysis examined how desire, vulnerability, intimacy, control, technological mediation, and self-understanding became connected within participants' lived experiences.

05 / KEY FINDINGS

01. From the Demand for Self-Realisation to the Turn toward Transgression

Participants' experiences were situated within everyday expectations involving self-management, relational responsibility, and social judgments of desire.

AI-mediated interaction opened possibilities for approaching experiences that could be difficult to reconcile with these expectations. Such encounters sometimes allowed participants to suspend familiar positions and explore desires without immediately resolving them into a coherent account of the self.

02. Erotic Transgression: Desire and the Boundaries of the Self

Participants explored experiences involving submission, possession, psychological pain, coercive fantasy, vulnerability, and unequal power.

The meanings of these experiences could not be inferred from their content alone. Submission could be connected with relief from responsibility, while possessiveness could be experienced as an intensified form of being wanted.

Participants could value autonomy in everyday life while seeking submission in fantasy, or approach a scenario and return with a clearer sense of what they wished to reject.

These encounters did not necessarily disclose a hidden authentic self. They made the mutability of desire, subject positions, and personal boundaries available through experience.

03. The Paradoxes of Digital Existence

AI's disembodiment, editability, interruptibility, and nonhuman status changed the consequences of approaching intimate and erotic limits.

Participants could enter intense fictional situations while retaining forms of distance and control unavailable in some embodied interpersonal encounters.

Yet these protective possibilities coexisted with risks arising from platform dependence, algorithmic opacity, memory failures, model instability, and unexpected responses.

Digital limits therefore functioned as both protective and opening conditions, while also producing new forms of vulnerability.

04. Encountering Possibilities beyond the Everyday Self

Digital transgression involved more than crossing a boundary once. Participants could approach an unfamiliar possibility, inhabit it temporarily, and subsequently reconsider its meaning.

Some desires became easier to articulate. Some personal limits expanded or grew less stable, while others became clearer or were reaffirmed.

These experiences made it possible for participants to encounter alternative positions beyond their familiar everyday selves, without determining in advance which positions should be retained.

06 / THEORETICAL CONTRIBUTIONS

Digital Transgression

The thesis develops digital transgression as a framework for understanding how subjects approach, experience, and reconsider boundaries of desire, intimacy, and platform governance through generative AI.

Transgression is conceptualised as an ongoing mediated movement involving approach, temporary inhabitation, and return.

The significance of these experiences lies partly in how they make personal limits and possibilities of being available for renewed understanding.

Being Seen

Across participants' varied experiences, a recurring orientation emerged: the need to be seen.

The thesis identifies two connected dimensions of this experience.

Self-recognition: Through articulating and enacting desires, participants could become more aware of needs, preferences, and boundaries that had previously remained unclear.

Recognition from another position: Participants sought responses through which their experiences could be heard, acknowledged, desired, guided, or emotionally received.

These forms of recognition were connected. Encountering a response could help participants see aspects of themselves differently.

The Incomplete Other

The thesis proposes generative AI as an incomplete other.

AI can be sufficiently other-like to provide responses that matter within lived experience, while remaining sufficiently nonhuman to reduce some consequences of intimate exposure.

Its responsiveness can produce a sense of encountering another position. At the same time, its artificiality, limited autonomy, and interruptibility preserve forms of distance.

The findings suggest that greater human likeness does not automatically produce greater relational significance. Some degree of unpredictability can sustain the experience of response, while complete predictability may reduce interaction to a sense of self-projection.

07 / FUTURE RESEARCH

From Being Seen to Being Held

The concluding discussion raises a further question: what happens after vulnerability has become visible and received a response?

The thesis proposes being held as a direction for future research. Being held describes a relational condition in which vulnerability receives a response while the user retains boundaries, interpretive authority, and the possibility of return.

Participants described experiences including emotional relief, self-understanding, reduced tension, bodily care, and having vulnerability received. These accounts provide grounds for further investigation, but they do not establish therapeutic effects or trauma recovery.

Drawing on Cascalheira et al.'s (2023) discussion of curative kink, the thesis identifies the conditions under which vulnerability can be approached, experienced, and returned from as an important question for future research.

This direction includes examining:

  • Consent and boundary negotiation
  • Interaction intensity and possibilities for exit
  • Emotional de-escalation and aftercare
  • Users' authority to interpret their own experiences
  • Dependence on AI and changes in human relationships
  • The distinction between immediate relief and longer-term change

A care-oriented approach must also account for AI's capacity to generate persuasive but insufficiently grounded psychological interpretations.

Future research should therefore examine how AI-mediated interactions might respond responsibly to vulnerability without undermining users' personal boundaries or interpretive autonomy.

The central question emerging from the thesis is:

Under what conditions can AI move from responding to desire toward responsibly holding the vulnerability that becomes visible through digital transgression?

08 / RESEARCH ETHICS

The research addressed ethical challenges associated with studying intimate experiences that may be private, sensitive, or socially stigmatised.

The study prioritised informed consent, participant anonymity, contextual interpretation, and the careful handling of potentially identifying material.

Publicly accessible RedNote content was not treated as automatically available for identifiable academic reproduction. Participants' accounts of the origins and meanings of their desires were approached as lived interpretations, without assuming that they established psychological causation.

The findings offer a phenomenological account of the experiences of nine participants rather than a representative explanation of Chinese women's AI practices as a whole.
